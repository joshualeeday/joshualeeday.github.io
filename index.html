About Me

Hi, my name is Joshua. Growing up with different game consoles, I not only like to play video games but also want to work as a 3d modeler so I can design and create objects for video games. To gain more knowledge and experience, I joined Arizona State University (ASU)’s Video Gaming Club more than 4 years ago.

In the club, I have worked with more than 100 club members and 20 plus art designers to create several video games. I am a graduate of ASU in Graphic Information Technology, where I also pursued the Gaming certificate. I have created 3d models and animation using several tools: Unity, Blender, Maya, 3ds Max, and MotionBuilder.

Kangaroo Rig & Animation

I rigged the kangaroo from my character project and animated it walking, running, and dancing in 3ds Max. The rig uses a CAT (Character Animation Tool) skeleton bound with the Skin modifier. I cleaned up the deformation by editing envelopes and painting vertex weights in the Weight Table, then built the walk and run cycles on CAT motion layers.

Walk Cycle in MotionBuilder

A walk cycle keyed by hand in MotionBuilder, the animation tool used across film and game studios. I added auxiliary pivots at the toe, foot base, and heel so the feet roll naturally, keyed the four main poses (contact, squash, passing, and up), mirrored them for the opposite leg, and refined the cycle to walk in place.

Motion Capture in MotionBuilder

Raw motion capture data brought to life on a character in MotionBuilder. I imported the MoCap data, mapped its markers to an Actor so the point cloud drives the actor’s skeleton frame by frame, then transferred the motion to a character and exported it as FBX, which I tested in Unity.
